\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This thesis examines whether insider ownership predicts future stock returns
in a global sample of 1{,}285 listed firms over January 2015 to December 2025.
Using the Fama-French five-factor model applied to ownership-sorted quintile
portfolios, I find that all portfolios earn positive and significant
risk-adjusted returns. The high-ownership portfolio (P5) generates an alpha of
$1.45\%$ per month ($17.5\%$ per year, $t = 4.93$). The high-minus-low (HML)
spread earns $0.53\%$ per month ($t = 2.73$), robust to adding momentum
(FF6: $0.528\%$, $t = 2.79$) and to value-weighting. Regional analysis shows
the premium is concentrated in North America (HML $= {+}1.768\%$ pm, $t=1.68$),
while Europe shows no significant spread ($-0.133\%$ pm, $t=-0.47$). Results
survive split-sample and alternative-sort robustness checks.

\vspace{6pt}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} insider ownership, stock returns,
Fama-French five-factor model, portfolio sorts, agency theory.\\
\noindent\textbf{JEL:} G12, G30, G34.
\end{abstract}
